СВОДКА · §0 Summary

Fourteen tokens, three frame weights, one accent at a time

This is the terminal half of the g2 design system. It binds three codebases: glory-to-gensek (ratatui), glory-to-protocol (Rich and Textual) and the review surface they hand off to (tuicr). They share one themes.toml shape, one glyph vocabulary and one voice. Every example on this page is painted only with the tokens in §1, so the paper toggle above re-renders the whole terminal.

ТРИ ПРАВИЛА · the three rules

1. The terminal tier. Uppercase is semantic (a focused title, a directory, a stamp), one accent is live at a time, the status line is always there. Lean KOI-7 console, not CP866 desktop.

2. The frame says who is speaking. Light for a pane at rest, heavy for the pane with focus, double for the bureau itself.

3. Tokens only. UI code never names a colour. It names accent, heading, note. The theme loader does the rest, in OKLab, at boot.

ТОКЕНЫ · §1 Tokens

The gensek fourteen, and how they map

Gensek's UI speaks fourteen semantic tokens. The design system's wider table (surface ramp, bright variants, the split red) folds into them as shown. A theme is the master trio plus the hand-fixed heading and note; the loader derives everything else and lifts each token until it clears 4.5:1 on bg.

ЗАМЕТКА · the contrast guard

At load, if note sits within 30° of accent in hue, or accent within 20° and 0.15 lightness of fg, the loader rotates note to the farthest of three candidate hues and logs a warning. The guard is skipped for grey accents. This is the blog's WCAG 1.4.1 rule run at boot instead of in a script.

ГЛИФЫ · §2 Glyphs

The vocabulary, and its ASCII fallback

CP866 kept the whole pseudographic block, so Russian terminals never lost their frames. The suite uses the Unicode box-drawing set with three weights, a handful of markers and the shade blocks. Every glyph has a 7-bit fallback for terminals that cannot draw it; the fallback is chosen once at boot, never mixed.

ШАПКА · §3 Header band

Logo, star, session line

Every full-screen view opens with a double-frame band: the bureau name tracked in accent, the star in red, then a session line in muted (date, connector freshness, locale). Protocol ships two logo sizes; the small one is the tracked word, the large one is a block-letter figure for the fallback console. On narrow terminals the band drops to one line.

ПАНЕЛИ · §4 Panels and screens

The dashboard and the object screen

Gensek's two screens, drawn to the standard. Panels are numbered in their title so g1…g3 jump to them. The focused panel is heavy and uppercase; the others rest. Section markers ▌ are red; the cursor row is washed in selection; notes are washed in note_bg with their label in sage.

ПЕЧАТЬ · §5 Stamps

Four decisions as text blocks

A terminal cannot tilt a ring, so the stamp is a heavy block: three lines, the decision in both languages, the label and detail beneath, right-aligned inside the form it decides. The glyph goes first. The colour is the decision's, as on the GUI. One stamp per form; show_result_stamp=False hides it, the dispatch still runs.

ПРАВИЛА · rules

Glyph, Cyrillic, English, detail. Four lines inside a heavy frame in the decision's colour.

Right-aligned in an 80-column form, flush with the frame's right edge; left-aligned if the terminal is narrower than 60.

Colours never swap. Approve is ok, reject bad, order info, review warn. A red approve is a bug.

Fallback: [v] APPROVED, [x] REJECTED, [!] DIRECT ORDER, [?] FOR REVIEW, in a +=+ frame.

ФОРМА · §6 Forms, confirms, directives

Eighty columns, dot leaders, the question as a ledger

Protocol's Form is the atom: a titled frame, 80 columns wide (FORM_WIDTH), lines of text or FIELD ....: value rows, and a stamp at the end. A confirm is a small form in a double frame whose answer is two keys. Help is a directive: the same form, with the bindings as its ledger.

ПОДВАЛ · §7 Footer, status line, ticker

The last line is always there

Inherited from Norton Commander's ten slots and still the fastest way to say what the keyboard does. The suite shows keys as inverted chips with a muted word, left; locale and sync state, right. The command line replaces the footer while open. A running job gets a one-line ticker above the footer, five squares cycling through the theme's colours.

СПИСКИ · §8 Lists and tables

Cursor, wash, and one state glyph per row

A list row is: cursor slot, identifier in accent or plain, title truncated with …, state glyph if any, age in muted at the right edge. The row under the cursor gets ▸ and the selection wash. A table is a list with aligned columns and a muted uppercase header; numbers are right-aligned.

ТЕМЫ · §9 Themes

Bureau is canonical; paper is a choice; the rest ship with gensek

The same dashboard excerpt in the two themes this repo owns, plus two that gensek ships from the blog. The toggle at the top of the page repaints every other example; these four are pinned so they can be compared. Legacy palettes (protocol's slate, gensek's teal) stay in their repos until each migrates.

ВНЕ КАНОНА · §10 Out of canon

What the terminal does not do

Most of the GUI list applies here too. These are the terminal-specific ways to turn the standard into a costume.

✗Emoji as state or decorationFour glyphs, ✓ ✗ ! ?, each with a word beside it.
✗Nerd Font icons as a hard dependencyBox drawing and blocks only, with a 7-bit fallback chosen once at boot.
✗Mixing single and double lines inside one frameOne weight per frame. Weight is the message.
✗Two heavy frames on screenExactly one focus. The rest rest.
✗Colour as the only carrier of stateGlyph plus word plus colour. Monochrome terminals still read it.
✗Blinking, marquee, spinners that never stopFive squares cycle while a job runs, and stop when it stops.
✗ASCII-art faces, banners on every commandOne header band per screen. The large logo only on the fallback console.
✗Hex codes in UI codeTokens only. theme.accent, never #d4af37.
✗Dropping to $EDITOR for a one-line noteThe inline box under the cursor, vim keys optional.
✗Cyrillic-only strings with no EnglishEvery UI string exists in en, pt-br and ru. The state's labels pair with the citizen's language.
